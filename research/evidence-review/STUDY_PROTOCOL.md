# Proposed study of evidence access and review accountability

Status: design proposal, not preregistered, recruited, approved, or executed. No participant data or human-study findings exist in this repository.

## Question and mechanisms

Does access to source evidence improve rejection of flawed financial recommendations, and does an expectation of explaining a decision change that effect? Evidence may enable scrutiny, but may also create unwarranted reassurance. Accountability could encourage inspection or simply increase effort. These are competing possibilities, not established findings.

## Candidate design

A between-participant 2 x 2 randomized design would vary (1) linked evidence unavailable versus available and (2) a routine decision versus an explicit expectation of justifying it to another reviewer. Recommendation text, financial facts, visual prominence and time limits should be held constant. Correctness would vary within participant across distinct cases. No participant should see two variants of the same base case. Counterbalance order and keep correctness rates fixed across arms.

Use an independent practice item and a brief pre-treatment financial-knowledge measure. Human reviewers must independently validate task ground truth, realism and the accept/reject/defer rubric before data collection. Do not use the simplistic diagnostic policy's score as a proxy for human performance. Measure whether evidence was opened and understood as manipulation/process checks, not as post-treatment covariates in the primary intention-to-treat estimate.

## Outcomes and analysis decisions to freeze

The primary outcome would be acceptance of faulty recommendations, with the denominator restricted to faulty trials. Also report rejection of correct recommendations, task accuracy under the fixed rubric, deferral rate, review time and confidence. Keep appropriate reliance distinct from subjective trust. Analyze correct and faulty trials separately rather than letting many easy correct cases inflate a headline accuracy.

Fit a pre-specified model appropriate to repeated binary outcomes, with participant and case effects and the evidence-by-accountability interaction. Treat domain-knowledge interactions as exploratory unless separately powered. Set sample size through simulation using a justified smallest effect of interest; do not invent a sample count. Record exclusions, missing responses, timeouts, multiplicity handling and uncertainty intervals before examining outcomes. Availability of evidence is not equivalent to its use.

## Boundaries

Obtain the applicable institutional ethics determination, consent procedure and data-management plan before recruiting. An unaffiliated portfolio prototype is not institutional research approval. Do not publish identifying participant records. Have a faculty mentor review the design and determine whether a controlled experiment, field study, or narrower technical validation is appropriate.

## Reading foundation

Buçinca, Malaya and Gajos (2021), [To Trust or to Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09692), reports a human-AI study on cognitive forcing and overreliance, including a usability trade-off. It motivates distinguishing decision quality from preferences; this proposal is not a replication and does not use its effects as power assumptions.

Rai (2020), [Explainable AI: from black box to glass box](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11747-019-00710-5), is a conceptual reading candidate. Publisher metadata was checked; full text has not been reviewed for this portfolio, so no specific mechanism is attributed to it here.
